# Patterns to Detect

## Core Template

The main pattern is a negation followed by a corrective assertion.

- `not X but Y`
- `not because X, but because Y`
- `not X. Y.`
- `the issue is not X, but Y`
- `the point is not X, but Y`

This pattern often sounds decisive, but it frequently works by inventing a weak `X` so that `Y` appears sharper than it is.

## Common Variants

### Direct Reversal

- `This is not X; it is Y.`
- `X is not the problem. Y is.`
- `The answer is not X. It is Y.`
- `The question is not whether X, but whether Y.`

### Comparative Reframe

- `less X, more Y`
- `more X than Y`
- `not simply X, but Y`
- `not merely X, but also Y`

These variants can be weaker signals than the direct template, but repeated use still creates AI-rhetorical cadence.

### Negation Ladder

- `not X, not Y, but Z`
- `it was not X or Y. It was Z.`

This structure is stronger rhetoric than analysis in most thesis prose.

### Fake Nuance Pattern

- `The issue is not X alone, but Y`
- `This is not only about X, but about Y`

These sometimes pretend to add nuance while still collapsing the relationship between X and Y into a template contrast.

## False-Binary Signals

Flag the sentence more aggressively when:

- `X` is a straw alternative
- both `X` and `Y` are actually true
- the author later reintroduces `X` as relevant
- the sentence treats emphasis as exclusion

## Usually Better Moves

Prefer one of these instead:

- state `Y` directly
- say that `Y` is the main concern in this case
- explain the relationship between `X` and `Y`
- replace reversal with a specific claim about scope, mechanism, or emphasis

## Exceptions

Do not force removal when the contrast carries real conceptual work, for example:

- distinguishing two theories or definitions
- rejecting an explicit claim made by a cited author
- clarifying a methodological boundary
- correcting a likely reader misunderstanding that materially affects interpretation

Even in those cases, check whether the sentence can be phrased more directly.
