# Before/After Examples

## Example 1: Empty Rhetorical Reframe

**Before:**
> "The issue is not visibility, but authenticity."

**After:**
> "Authenticity is the central concern in this case."

**Why flag it:** The sentence uses `visibility` as a setup term without explaining why it must be rejected.

---

## Example 2: False Binary

**Before:**
> "Influencer success is not about talent but strategy."

**After:**
> "Influencer success in the sampled cases depends less on individual talent alone than on repeatable strategic self-presentation."

**Why flag it:** The original sets up a false exclusion. The revision keeps the emphasis without pretending talent is irrelevant.

---

## Example 3: Staged Contrast Rhythm

**Before:**
> "This is not a personal story. It is a platform story."

**After:**
> "This section analyzes how platform dynamics shape what appears as personal storytelling."

**Why flag it:** The original uses a neat reversal for effect. The revision states the analytical move directly.

---

## Example 4: Valid Distinction, Better Form

**Before:**
> "The question is not whether creators perform authenticity, but how they operationalize it across formats."

**After:**
> "This chapter examines how creators operationalize authenticity across formats."

**Why flag it:** The underlying distinction is valid, but the rhetorical setup is unnecessary.

---

## Example 5: Contrast That Should Probably Stay

**Before:**
> "The distinction here is not between truth and falsehood, but between sincerity and strategic disclosure."

**After:**
> "The distinction here is between truth and falsehood on one hand and sincerity and strategic disclosure on the other."

**Why flag it:** This may be a real conceptual distinction, so the issue is phrasing, not the contrast itself. The rewrite removes the template while preserving the theoretical boundary.
