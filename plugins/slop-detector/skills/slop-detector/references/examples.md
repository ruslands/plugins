# Before/After Examples

## Example 1: Empty Academic Weight

**Before:**
> "This finding is significant because it highlights the importance of authenticity in digital communication."

**After:**
> "This finding suggests that authenticity functions as a visible criterion of audience evaluation in digital communication."

**Why flag it:** The original repeats `significant` and `importance` without specifying the finding's actual implication.

---

## Example 2: Transition Stack

**Before:**
> "Moreover, it is important to note that creators also use vulnerability strategically."

**After:**
> "Creators also use vulnerability strategically."

**Why flag it:** The transition and announcement phrase add no content.

---

## Example 3: Template Contrast

**Before:**
> "The issue is not visibility, but trust."

**After:**
> "Trust is the central concern in this case."

**Why flag it:** The sentence uses a staged contrast where a direct claim would be stronger.

---

## Example 4: Generic Paragraph Logic

**Before:**
> "Platform communication is a crucial part of influencer culture. It shapes how audiences respond to content. For example, creators often manage tone carefully. Therefore, platform communication is highly important."

**After:**
> "Platform communication shapes audience response by controlling tone, pacing, and perceived spontaneity. In the sampled videos, creators use these cues to make strategic disclosures appear informal."

**Why flag it:** The original follows a stock claim-elaboration-example-wrap-up pattern and ends where it should have started.

---

## Example 5: Empty Conclusion Sentence

**Before:**
> "Taken together, these findings underscore the importance of strategic authenticity."

**After:**
> "Taken together, these findings suggest that strategic authenticity is presented as both an emotional style and a commercial practice."

**Why flag it:** The rewrite keeps the conclusion move but replaces vague evaluative language with the actual claim.
