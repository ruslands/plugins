# Lexical Signals

## Empty Importance Language

These are not automatically wrong, but they are common in generic AI-academic prose when left unsupported.

- "significant"
- "important"
- "crucial"
- "key"
- "vital"
- "central"
- "complex"
- "problematic"
- "robust"
- "nuanced"

Flag them when the sentence does not specify: significant in what sense, for whom, by what measure, or under what conditions.

## Generic Academic Filler

- "It is important to note that"
- "It is worth noting that"
- "This highlights the importance of"
- "This underscores the need for"
- "In the context of this study"
- "From this perspective"
- "It can be argued that"
- "One might argue that"
- "It is evident that"

These often announce seriousness without adding content.

## Inflated Diction

- "utilize"
- "leverage"
- "facilitate"
- "elucidate"
- "multifaceted"
- "paradigm"
- "holistic"
- "catalyze"
- "juxtapose"
- "realm"

Do not ban these blindly. Flag them when a simpler word would carry the same meaning with less performance.

## Overused Transitions

- "Furthermore"
- "Moreover"
- "Additionally"
- "Notably"
- "Importantly"
- "Interestingly"
- "Consequently"
- "In conclusion"

One is normal. Repeated use across adjacent paragraphs is a signal.

## Weak Certainty and Weak Hedging

Flag when these appear without purpose:

- "clearly"
- "obviously"
- "undeniably"
- "somewhat"
- "relatively"
- "arguably"
- "it seems that"
- "may potentially"
- "could possibly"

The issue is mismatch between wording and evidence, not the existence of qualifiers by itself.
