# Structural Signals

## Reversal Templates

Common forms:

- `not X but Y`
- `the issue is not X, but Y`
- `this is not A. It is B.`
- `less X, more Y`
- `not merely X, but Y`

Flag when the contrast creates rhetorical force without analytical necessity.

## Announcement Sentences

- "This section will explore..."
- "The purpose of this chapter is to..."
- "What this shows is..."
- "The key point here is that..."

These are often weaker than simply making the point.

## Paragraph Template Repetition

Watch for paragraphs that repeat this sequence:

1. broad claim
2. vague elaboration
3. safe example
4. wrap-up sentence about significance

One paragraph may be fine. Repetition across a chapter is a slop signal.

## Symmetry Addiction

Flag suspicious balance such as:

- three nearly identical sub-points
- equal-length paragraphs with identical openings
- repeated claim/contrast/conclusion cadence

Human revision usually leaves unevenness where the argument requires it.

## List Abuse

Flag lists when they replace explanation rather than organize information:

- every bullet starts with the same verb
- bullets restate headings
- the list could be one sentence
- the list hides the need for analysis

## Generic Conclusion Moves

- "Taken together, these findings highlight the importance of..."
- "Ultimately, this demonstrates that..."
- "In conclusion, it is evident that..."

These often summarize in empty terms rather than naming the result or implication.
